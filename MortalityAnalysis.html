Relationship between GDP per capita and life expectancy

Interpretation

Countries with higher GDP per capita in 2020 tend to have higher life expectancy. The scatterplot shows a strong positive association: wealthier economies cluster in the upper-right (high income and long lives), while lower-income countries are concentrated toward shorter life expectancy.

The relationship is not perfectly linear. At low income levels, gains in GDP are often accompanied by relatively large improvements in longevity (basic health, nutrition, and sanitation). Among already high-income countries, additional GDP growth is associated with smaller marginal gains in life expectancy, producing a pattern of diminishing returns.

The world maps highlight similar geographic patterns for both indicators: much of sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia show lower GDP and lower life expectancy, whereas North America, Western Europe, East Asia, and Oceania rank higher on both measures. Discrepancies remain—some middle-income countries achieve life expectancy comparable to richer nations through strong public health systems, while a few high-income outliers sit below the trend because of inequality, diet, or other non-economic factors.
